A customer in your town types “plumber Namur” or “hairdresser in Liège”. They are not looking for an explanation, they want someone now. What helps them choose between you and the business next door is often what they find on Google in a few seconds: your local visibility. Good news: you can work on it, with simple, regular actions.

## Why your Google Business Profile is your local customers' first stop

When someone searches for a tradesperson in their town, Google often shows a map with a few businesses highlighted, before the regular websites: this is the “local pack”. Not appearing there means missing some of the customers searching near you.

Your Google Business Profile is the foundation of your local search visibility. It carries your name, opening hours, photos, reviews and phone number. It is free, and it is often the first thing a customer sees of you, even before your website.

## The 5 practical settings to get right on your profile

### 1. Choose the right main category

“Plumber” is not “plumbing works”. The main category tells Google which searches to link you to. Pick the one that describes your trade the way your customers would say it, then add two or three useful secondary categories.

### 2. Fill in the profile completely

A half-empty profile makes people hesitate. Exact address, service area, phone number, website, a short and concrete description: everything should be there. Google itself recommends filling in every field, and a complete profile answers customers' questions before they need to ask.

### 3. Add recent photos

Three photos are enough to start: your vehicle or your premises, a before and after of a job, your team. Add one or two a month. Recent photos show an active tradesperson and give a concrete idea of your work.

### 4. Publish your opening hours and a tap-to-call number

A customer who is unsure about your hours calls a competitor. Up-to-date hours, bank holidays, a number that can be tapped to call straight from the phone: every detail sorted makes calling you easier.

### 5. Look after your first reviews

Ask for a review after every finished job, with a direct link sent by text message. Reply to every review, including bad ones, calmly and in plain words. Google itself says that the number of reviews and the rating count in local ranking.

## What happens every month: reviews, directories and website pages to follow

An up-to-date profile is the foundation. But local search visibility is not sorted once and for all: it needs upkeep. Three fronts move at the same time, every month.

Reviews: new reviews, up-to-date replies, a steady rhythm rather than a spike followed by nothing.

Directories: trade directories, local platforms, professional bodies. Your details must be identical everywhere: same name, same number, same address. Details that contradict each other create doubt, for your customers as well as for Google.

Your website pages: one page per service, for example “heating repairs”, with your real service area, your real opening hours and your real work.

This is where many tradespeople fall behind. Not for lack of goodwill, but for lack of time. The weeks go by, the profile stops moving and the ranking drops back.

## How long before you see results?

Local search visibility builds over several months, not a few days, and nobody can guarantee a position. Rhythm matters more than a one-off effort: a little time every week is better than one big session every six months.

Your local competitors update their profiles too: a profile kept up regularly keeps its details reliable.
