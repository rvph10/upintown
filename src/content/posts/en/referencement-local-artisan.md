A customer in your town types “plumber Namur” or “hairdresser in Liège”. They are not looking for an explanation, they want someone now. What decides whether they call you or your neighbour is almost always one thing: your local visibility. Good news: you can work on it, with simple, regular actions.

## Why your Google Business Profile is your local customers' first stop

When someone searches for a tradesperson in their town, Google first shows three results on a map: this is the “local pack”. Three places, not ten. If you are not there, you do not exist for that customer, even with the best website in the world.

Your Google Business Profile is the foundation of your local search visibility. It carries your name, opening hours, photos, reviews and phone number. It is free, and for many tradespeople it brings in more calls than the website itself. It is also the first thing most people look at before deciding.

## The 5 practical settings that help a tradesperson rise in their town

### 1. Choose the right main category

“Plumber” is not “plumbing works”. The main category tells Google which searches to link you to. Pick the one that describes your trade the way your customers would say it, then add two or three useful secondary categories.

### 2. Fill in the profile 100%

A half-empty profile makes people hesitate. Exact address, service area, phone number, website, a short and concrete description: everything should be there. Google favours complete profiles, and a reassured customer calls faster than a doubtful one.

### 3. Add recent photos

Three photos are enough to start: your vehicle or your premises, a before and after of a job, your team. Add one or two a month. Profiles with recent photos get noticeably more contacts, because they show an active tradesperson.

### 4. Publish your opening hours and a tap-to-call number

A customer who is unsure about your hours calls a competitor. Up-to-date hours, bank holidays, a number that can be tapped to call straight from the phone: every bit of friction removed is one more call.

### 5. Look after your first reviews

Ask for a review after every finished job, with a direct link sent by text message. Reply to every review, including bad ones, calmly and in plain words. The number and freshness of reviews weigh heavily in the local ranking.

## What happens every month: reviews, directories and website pages to follow

An up-to-date profile is the foundation. But local search visibility is not sorted once and for all: it needs upkeep. Three fronts move at the same time, every month.

Reviews: new reviews, up-to-date replies, a steady rhythm rather than a spike followed by nothing.

Directories: trade directories, local platforms, professional bodies. Your details must be identical everywhere: same name, same number, same address. Inconsistencies lower your credibility in Google's eyes.

Your website pages: one page per town or per service, for example “heating repairs in Charleroi”, with your real opening hours and your real work.

This is where many tradespeople fall behind. Not for lack of goodwill, but for lack of time. The weeks go by, the profile stops moving and the ranking drops back.

## How long before you see results?

On a new profile, allow a few weeks for the first calls, and two to three months for a stable position in your town. Rhythm matters more than a one-off effort: ten minutes every week is better than an afternoon every six months.

One thing to watch: local competition never stands still. If you stop, you do not stay where you are, you slip back.

## In short

Your local visibility is not a matter of luck. Go back to your Google Business Profile, apply the five settings, then look after it every month: reviews, directories and website pages. That is how a tradesperson shows up at the top of Google in their town, and stays there.
