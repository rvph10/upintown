You need a website for your small business, and three routes are open to you. The first is to build it yourself. The second is to go through a traditional agency. The third is to hand everything over to an all-inclusive service at a fixed price. Each has a real cost, in money, in time and in energy. Here is what each option really asks of you, so that you can choose with your eyes open.

## Building your website yourself: little outlay at the start, a lot of time afterwards

The idea is appealing. You sign up to an online tool, pick a template and fill in the pages. The first few euros you spend stay modest, and you keep control of every detail.

The time, though, is paid for in another way. You have to choose the hosting (the service that keeps your site online) and the domain name, set up professional email addresses, write the text, prepare the images, then check that everything displays properly on a phone as well as on a computer.

Then comes the part people talk about less. A website does not stay in place on its own. Technical updates keep coming, forms must keep working, and someone has to answer the emails that visitors send from the site.

For a self-employed person or a very small business, this work often lands in the evenings and at weekends. It is not a question of skill, but of availability. Between two client jobs, the website comes after the day's urgent matters, and going live drags on for months.

Doing it yourself remains a sensible choice when you enjoy technical work and want to learn. It becomes harder when your trade lies elsewhere: advising, caring, building, repairing.

## Letting AI generate the site: very fast, but it needs checking

A generator can produce a layout, images and text in moments from a short description. For testing an idea or publishing a simple page, that may be enough.

The work doesn't disappear, though. You still have to read every sentence, because the tool can invent a service or a turnaround, add your legal information, then look after everything yourself. The article on [choosing between an AI website and a professional](/en/blog/ai-website-or-professional) sets out what these tools solve and what they leave open.

## Going through a traditional agency: what it charges and why the quote comes as a surprise

Another route is to entrust the project to an agency. You benefit from solid skills, a carefully crafted design and tailored support. For a large project with special features, this approach makes sense.

The difficulty often appears when the quote arrives. A website delivered by an agency is built from many separate lines: design, text, integration, features, then, once the site is live, hosting, maintenance and changes. Each line has its price, and the final total sometimes exceeds what the self-employed owner had in mind.

Then comes day-to-day life. Every small correction, an opening time to adjust, a new photo to swap in, a paragraph to rewrite, goes through a formal request. The wait depends on the agency's schedule, and each job may be billed separately.

There is nothing improper about this way of working. It matches an organisation built for one-off projects. But for a small business that needs to change its website several times a year, it takes budget and patience.

Three points set this approach apart.

## How to decide, depending on your situation

These options are not mutually exclusive, but they suit different situations. If budget is your first question, start with [how much a website costs](/en/blog/website-cost), or [the case of a tradesperson](/en/blog/website-cost-tradesperson).

If you have time, you are interested in the technical side and your website can wait, doing it yourself remains possible. Count the real time, not only the starting budget.

If you want to test an idea quickly and will take the time to read everything, an AI tool may be enough to begin with.

If your project is large, with very specific needs, a traditional agency may suit. Then plan for the running budget, the cost of changes and care, not only the cost of the build.

If your priority is to stay focused on your trade, with the text written for you, a website online within a known timeframe and regular care at the same price, an all-inclusive service at a fixed price meets that need.

One simple question helps you decide: in six months, who will look after your website? If the answer is not you, you may as well decide that now.
