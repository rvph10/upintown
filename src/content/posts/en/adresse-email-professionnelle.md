A quote sent from a Gmail or Hotmail address works. But when a customer is choosing between two providers, an address like contact@your-business.com shows at a glance that your business is established. It's a small detail that affects trust, and you only have to set it up once.

## What your customers see

An address on your own domain reminds people of your business with every message. It matches your website, your Google Business Profile and your business cards. And it stays the same if you ever change email provider: what counts is your domain name, not the tool behind it.

It's practical too. You can create several addresses for different purposes (contact@, invoices@) and keep your work email clearly apart from your personal email.

## What you need to set up a professional address

Three things are needed:

1. **A domain name**, for example your-business.com. If you already have a website, you probably have one.
2. **An email service** that hosts your mailboxes: Zoho Mail, Google Workspace or Microsoft 365, for example.
3. **A few technical settings** on the domain, so messages reach the right mailbox and what you send is recognised as genuine.

That third point is where things most often go wrong.

## The settings that keep you out of spam folders

When you send an email, your customer's email service checks that it really comes from you. For that, your domain needs three settings, called SPF, DKIM and DMARC. Put simply, they are signatures proving your messages aren't fakes.

Without them, your quotes and invoices can end up in your customers' spam folders, and nobody tells you. Large email services like Gmail have become stricter with senders who don't have them. If you already have a professional address and some customers aren't receiving your messages, this is the first thing to check.

## Switching without losing anything

Moving from an old address to the new one takes a few steps:

- **Import your old messages** into the new mailbox, so your whole history stays in one place.
- **Forward the old address** to the new one for a few months, while everyone gets used to it.
- **Tell your regular customers** and add the new address to your signature.
- **Update your address everywhere**: website, Google Business Profile, social accounts, invoices and business cards.

Keep the old address active as long as messages still arrive there.
