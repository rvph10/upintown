Google reviews are often what a future customer reads just before calling you. They look at the rating, but also at how you reply. A calm reply to a negative review can reassure people more than a string of positive reviews left unanswered.

## Why reply to every review

Your replies are public. They are addressed to the person who left the review, but above all to everyone who reads it afterwards. They show that you're there, that you listen, and that you deal with problems when they happen.

Replying to every review, good or bad, also shows your business is active. A profile where the replies stopped months ago looks like a business that no longer checks it.

## Replying to a positive review

A short reply is enough, as long as it isn't copied and pasted everywhere:

- thank the person, by first name if they show it;
- mention a detail from their review, to show you read it;
- end simply, without a sales pitch.

> "Thank you Sophie for your feedback. We're glad you love the new kitchen, and thank you for making us so welcome during the work."

## Replying to a negative review

This is where it really counts. Take time to reread the review with a clear head before replying, then follow four steps:

1. **Thank** the person for taking the time to write.
2. **Acknowledge** how they feel, without necessarily agreeing with everything.
3. **Explain briefly** what happened or what has changed, without a long justification.
4. **Offer to continue privately**, by phone or email, to find a solution.

> "Thank you for taking the time to write to us. We understand your disappointment about the delay. We'd like to talk it through with you and find a solution: you can reach us on the number shown on our profile."

Future customers don't judge the negative review on its own. They judge how you reply to it.

## What to avoid

- **Replying in the heat of the moment**, in an argumentative tone.
- **Revealing information about the customer**: their address, the details of their order, your exchanges with them. Your replies are public, and that information remains personal.
- **Confirming that someone is your client**, if your profession is bound by confidentiality (lawyer, doctor, therapist). A general reply that confirms nothing is safer.
- **Offering a discount or a gift in exchange for a review.** Google prohibits reviews obtained in return for an incentive.

## What if the review is fake?

You can't delete a review yourself, even an unfair one. You can, however, report it to Google if it breaks its rules: advertising, unrelated to your business, left by a competitor, or offensive. Google then decides whether to remove it.

In the meantime, a short, factual reply is still useful: it shows readers that you don't recognise the situation described, without getting into a public exchange.
