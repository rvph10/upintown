Describe your business in a sentence, and moments later an AI tool offers you a complete website: layout, images and text. It is impressive, and sometimes it is enough. But a published website is more than what you see on screen. Here is what these tools do well, what they leave with you, and how to choose for your situation.

## What these tools do well

They are quick and cheap to start with. One mass-market offer, for instance, says [AI will create the layout, images and text from a description of your business](https://www.hostinger.com/be-fr/site-internet-professionnel), for €1.99 a month. Do read the terms, though: on the same page, that rate means paying €95.52 upfront for 48 months, then renewing at €9.99 a month. There is nothing unusual about that, but the headline price isn't the long-term price.

They also give you something concrete to react to instead of a blank page. That is useful even if your final site gets made another way.

## When an AI-generated site is enough

It may suit you perfectly well if:

- you are testing an idea or starting a side business, with little at stake
- your customers come mainly through word of mouth and just want to check you exist
- you need a simple page for a limited time
- you have the time to read everything, correct it and keep the site up to date yourself

In those cases, spending more won't necessarily help.

## What it leaves unsolved

### Accurate text

AI writes plausible sentences, not necessarily true ones. It can add a service you don't offer, invent experience or promise a turnaround you can't meet. It doesn't know your customers, your prices or what sets you apart.

Google makes the same point, calling it [critical to manually fact-check and review all AI-generated content before publishing](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/using-gen-ai-content). That job falls to you.

### Local search

Even a good website won't put you on Google by itself. For a search such as "plumber Liège", Google also looks at [your Business Profile, your reviews and how well known you are](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en). No site generator replies to your reviews or keeps your details consistent across directories. The article on [local visibility](/en/blog/local-google-visibility) covers this work.

### Email

Your website and your [professional email address](/en/blog/professional-email-address) rely on the same domain name. Some tools offer email as an extra and others don't. Check who sets it up, and whether your messages will be recognised by your customers' mail services.

### Upkeep

Once the site is live, someone still has to change opening hours, replace a photo, renew the domain and react when something stops working. With a generator, that someone is you. Check what you can take with you too: if you leave the platform, will you have to rebuild the site? And is the domain in your name?

### Who answers for what it says

What appears on your website remains your responsibility, whichever tool wrote it. In Belgium, the Federal Public Service Economy [lists the information a business website must show](https://news.economie.fgov.be/203681-informations-obligatoires-sur-le-site-web-de-votre-entreprise/), including your name, your address, contact details with an email address and your company number. A generator doesn't know these, so adding and checking them is down to you. If you trade elsewhere, check the rules that apply there.

## Three approaches, side by side

| | AI alone | AI, then your own review | A professional |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starting cost | Low | Low | Higher |
| Your time | Little, but risky | A lot | A few approvals |
| Accurate text | Unchecked | If you check it | Checked with you |
| Google profile and reviews | Separate | Separate | Depends on the offer |
| Professional email | Check | Check | Depends on the offer |
| Upkeep | You | You | Depends on the offer |
| Who to contact in an outage | The tool's support | The tool's support | Your contact |

The choice isn't simply between a machine and a person. A cheap site made by someone can leave the same questions open, and a professional may well use these tools. What matters is who checks the result, and who answers afterwards.

## How to decide

Ask yourself four questions:

1. **Do customers find you on Google?** If so, a website alone won't be enough, whoever makes it.
2. **Would a mistake on your site have consequences?** In healthcare, law or construction, inaccurate text carries more weight.
3. **Who will look after it in six months?** If the answer isn't you, plan for that now.
4. **How much time can you give it?** Include reading and correcting.

If you do go with an AI-generated site, read every sentence before publishing, add your legal information, test the contact form on a phone and make sure the domain is in your name.

## In short

An AI-generated site suits an idea you want to test or a business that depends little on Google, as long as you take the time to read everything. It doesn't solve accurate text, local search, email or upkeep. To choose, don't just compare the first month's price: ask who checks the result, and who looks after it afterwards.
