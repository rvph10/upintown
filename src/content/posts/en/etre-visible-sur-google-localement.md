Among independents and small businesses, one question comes up again and again: how do you get found on Google by customers in your neighbourhood or town? The answer comes down to a few simple settings, then a presence that is looked after over time. UpInTown supports independent professionals and local services across French-speaking Belgium in this area. Here are the most common questions, with practical answers.

## Is the Google Business Profile the first place to start?

For many local searches, the business listing that Google displays is the first point of contact with your business. It shows your name, address, opening hours, photos and reviews. Before going further, this profile needs to be complete and accurate. A misspelt address, opening hours that have not been updated or a category that is too vague are enough to send the right people to the wrong information. So the first step is to claim the profile, fill in every field and keep it up to date.

## Which reviews really matter?

Reviews weigh on how a customer chooses, and they feed into local results. Three things count: how regularly new reviews arrive, the reply given to each one, and the fact that they come from real customers. Replying calmly, even to a mixed review, shows that you are reachable and reliable. Asking a satisfied customer for a review just after a job remains the simplest way to get one, with no tricks.

## Are local directories still useful?

Yes, as long as you are consistent. The Pages d'Or, professional directories and trade listings often reuse your business details. When the name, address and phone number differ from one site to the next, that inconsistency muddies the signals. The aim is not to be everywhere, but to be accurate everywhere you are already listed.

## Do you need a page for each town?

A page per town only makes sense if it brings real content: the areas you cover, examples of local work, an address or directions where relevant. Creating ten almost identical pages with only the name of the town changed adds nothing solid. It is better to have one clear page for each area where you really work, in Liège, Namur, Charleroi, Mons or Wavre, than a series of empty pages.

## How do you choose the words customers type?

Few people search for “local search optimisation”. They are more likely to type “office cleaning Namur”, “employment lawyer Liège” or “bathroom renovation Charleroi”. Spot the phrases your customers actually use, thinking about their need and their town. These words belong in the page title, the first paragraph, the section headings and your profile. Without overdoing it, and without jargon.

## How long does it take to see a change?

There is no guaranteed timescale. Local signals take time: a completed profile, regular reviews, consistent pages and identical details everywhere build up month after month. A fast website that is easy to read on a phone and kept up to date helps too. What matters is consistency rather than a one-off effort.

## What most often holds small businesses back?

Time, above all. Between customers, quotes and admin, the Google Business Profile, reviews and pages end up at the bottom of the list. The result: details that go out of date, a review left unanswered, a contact page that points to a deactivated email address. None of these is serious in itself, but put together, they cost you contacts who were simply trying to reach you.
