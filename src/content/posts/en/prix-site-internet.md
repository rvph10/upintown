Search for the cost of a website and you'll find everything from a few euros a month with a site builder to quotes of several thousand euros from an agency. Those numbers don't describe the same thing. To compare them, separate two costs: what you pay once to build the site, and what you pay every month to keep it online.

## What you pay once: the build

Building the site covers all the work done before launch. A serious quote breaks it into clear stages.

- **Planning**: understanding your business, your customers and what the site should help them do (call, request a quote, book).
- **Text**: often missing from quotes, yet it takes the most time. A site without clear text convinces nobody, however good it looks.
- **Design**: how the site looks, ideally approved on a mockup before the site is built.
- **Build and launch**: the complete site, tested on phones and computers, with a contact form and the legal pages.

If a quote doesn't list these stages, ask what it includes. Two very different prices are often explained by text being included in one and left to you in the other.

## What you pay every month: the site online

A website isn't something you buy and put away. To stay online and secure, it needs a few things, paid every year or every month:

- **The domain name** (your-business.com), renewed every year.
- **Hosting**, meaning the server your site runs on.
- **Security updates**, so a flaw doesn't take your site offline.
- **A professional email address**, often on the same domain.
- **Small changes**: new opening hours, a new price, a photo to replace.

Some providers bundle all of this into a monthly plan. Others bill each item separately, or leave the upkeep to you. Both approaches can work, as long as you know in advance who handles what.

## Site builders: cheaper to start?

Tools like Wix or Squarespace advertise a modest monthly subscription. They suit you if you have time to learn the tool, write your own text and keep everything up to date yourself. The real cost is that time, taken away from your actual work.

One thing to know before you start: a site built on these platforms stays with them. If you want to change provider later, the site usually has to be rebuilt.

## Questions to ask before signing

1. Who writes the text, and is it included in the price?
2. Will I see a mockup before the site is built?
3. How many rounds of corrections are included?
4. Will the domain name be registered in my name?
5. What happens if I want to change provider: do I get my site back?
6. Once the site is live, who handles updates and small changes, and at what price?

A provider who answers these six questions clearly saves you from most unpleasant surprises.
